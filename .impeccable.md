# Design Context

Used by the impeccable skill chain (`/audit`, `/distill`, `/harden`, `/arrange` etc) to avoid generic output. Update when the brand direction shifts.

## Target audience

UK adults checking water quality before swimming. Three primary personas:

1. **The casual visitor.** Parent or holidaymaker arriving at a beach in Cornwall or Northumberland, phone in hand, deciding whether to let the kids in the water. Mid-30s to 60s. Not technical. Reads headlines, not data tables.
2. **The wild and sea swimmer.** Year-round outdoor swimmer who already knows the EA's annual classification system and follows Surfers Against Sewage. Wants the underlying numbers, the recent sewage discharge history, the rainfall context. Will rapidly leave for SafeToDip or SSRS if the data is thin.
3. **The local resident or angler.** Checks one or two sites repeatedly. Wants a clear up-or-down answer and a no-nonsense reason. Trusts established institutions (GOV.UK, BBC, Environment Agency) more than apps.

All three trust editorial credibility over commercial polish. They are looking at the page for under thirty seconds.

## Use cases (jobs to be done)

- "I'm at Bournemouth, is it safe to swim today?"
- "Has there been a recent sewage discharge near [beach]?"
- "What's the official water-quality rating at [beach]?"
- "Find the nearest clean beach to me right now."
- "Show me water quality across Cornwall this morning."

The single most important moment is the verdict above the fold. Everything else is supporting evidence.

## Brand personality and tone

GOV.UK credibility filtered through editorial typography. Distinctive, not AI-generic. Reference points: Guardian weather page, BBC Weather, GOV.UK service pages, a printed MOT certificate, the Met Office shipping forecast.

The interface should feel **calm, confident, hand-set**. It should not look like:

- A modern SaaS landing page (gradient buttons, "modern SaaS" purple, glassmorphism)
- A startup app (rounded icons above every heading, hero metrics, centred three-column features)
- A generic data dashboard (dense tables, sparklines as decoration)

It should feel:

- Edited, not designed by a template
- Quietly opinionated (the verdict gives you a single answer, not five charts)
- Trustworthy by composition rather than by trust badges
- British (no commas before "and", no Oxford commas, no em dashes)

The verdict block is the centrepiece. It is set in a serif at editorial sizes and reads like a one-line front-page headline. The factor list below reads like a document, not a card grid.

## Visual system

**Type.** Iowan Old Style serif for display (with Source Serif Pro, Apple Garamond, Baskerville and Times New Roman as fallbacks). System sans for body. No Inter, no Roboto. Italic for marginalia and the brand wordmark's question mark.

**Palette.** Warm cream background (`#fbfaf6`), deep marine ink (`#0b1f2a`), tinted neutrals leaning toward the brand hue. Three earned accents only: forest green for Yes (`#1f6f4b`), darkened ochre for Caution (`#7a4a08`), brick red for No (`#9b2c2c`). All three appear together as a 4px stripe rule under the masthead. Dark mode is a proper variant, not an inversion.

**Layout.** Editorial rhythm. Generous vertical space at the top of each section. Asymmetry over grids where it earns its keep (see the methodology list with hanging roman numerals on the homepage). Card grids only for genuinely uniform content (featured locations, nearby beaches).

**Motion.** Sparing. Pulse animation on an ongoing discharge dot uses opacity. Hover transitions on cards use `transform: translateY(-1px)` only. A global `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` reset suppresses everything for users who ask.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

The audit is allergic to:

- Gradient text on metrics or headings
- Hero-metric template (big number, small label, supporting stats)
- Dot-separator stats strips ("Foo · Bar · Baz")
- Identical card grids with icon above heading repeated four times
- Glassmorphism, neon glow accents, AI cyan-on-dark palette
- Centred everything
- "Modal because we needed somewhere to put it"

## Constraints

- WCAG 2.1 AA across colour, contrast, focus, motion, touch targets (44px minimum).
- Core Web Vitals: aim for green. Initial JS chunk under 30 KB gzipped (currently ~22 KB).
- Mobile-first responsiveness with a clean break at 540-640px.
- Plain HTML works without JavaScript: the verdict is server-rendered.
- All copy must follow the British-English rules in CLAUDE.md.

## Differentiation

What people should remember about this site:

1. **The single confident verdict.** Yes, Caution or No, with a one-line reason. No competitor frames it this way.
2. **The three-stripe rule** running under the masthead, echoed in the tone-coloured rule under the verdict headline.
3. **Editorial typography** on a water-quality answer page — readers expect a database, they get a document.
4. **UK-wide coverage** with one consistent treatment across England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.
